Website Name Ideas & Domain Check

Compiled 2026-07-08 · 174 candidate names checked live across .com / .dev / .app / .build / .codes (+ a 10-name .io spot check + a 76-name B2B SaaS collision check)

Found real, registrable options. The first two rounds checked 54 plain-English words on .com/.dev/.app/.io and came back 100% taken — that's normal, single dictionary words on those TLDs have been squatted for decades. Widening the search to .build and .codes (much smaller, far-less-squatted registries) and adding 22 coined/product-style names (in the spirit of "OpenClaw" / "T3 Chat") turned up dozens of open domains — including two names free on all five extensions at once, .com included. A fourth round tried -ly/-us/-ify suffixes on the strongest words — .com turned out to be fully squatted for this trick too, but six suffix names are wide open everywhere else. A fifth round went after more advanced/obscure CS jargon (concurrency, distributed systems, compilers, git internals, ML) — .com was a clean sweep of "taken" again, but .build/.codes still delivered. A sixth round added 50 more advanced terms and, critically, ran a B2B SaaS brand-collision check on all 76 advanced terms from rounds 5+6 — turns out 24 of 26 round-5 names and 37 of 50 round-6 names collide with a real, currently-operating company (mostly cybersecurity, fintech, and AI SaaS firms). Every collided name got a fallback search: 4 common prefixes (Get-/Try-/Use-/Run-) and 4 suffixes (-ly/-us/-ify/-io) checked for a clean, collision-avoiding .com. See Available Now and Collision Check below.

Will any of these get flagged "Premium"?

Short answer: no, very unlikely for any name on this page. "Premium" pricing is a surcharge some registries/registrars apply at first registration to strings they've algorithmically judged especially valuable — mainly very short strings (1–4 characters), plain dictionary words, and numeric domains. Every available name in this report is a multi-syllable invented word or a real word + suffix (Craftus, ForgeClaw, Q3Code, etc.) — exactly the profile that registry premium-scoring engines do not flag. Spot searches for a few of the strongest picks (Craftus.dev, Sparkus.app, Compilus.build) turned up no premium listings anywhere.

Caveat: live checkout verification (GoDaddy's actual cart, which shows a "Premium" badge + price directly) needs browser automation, and this session's automation credits ran out mid-research — so this is a well-grounded estimate from registry pricing patterns and search spot-checks, not a screenshot of a checkout page. Before registering, a 10-second sanity check is free: search the name directly on godaddy.com or namecheap.com — if it's premium, the elevated price is shown immediately, no purchase needed.

Don't confuse this with aftermarket pricing (below) — that's a completely different thing: a domain that's already registered, being resold by its current owner. Aftermarket prices ($4,599 for Diff.dev, etc.) have nothing to do with registry premium tiers.

Category 5 — Advanced / obscure dev terms

Past the everyday vocabulary (fork, deploy, cache) into more insider CS jargon: concurrency primitives (Mutex, Barrier), distributed-systems terms (Raft, Paxos, Quorum, Gossip, Shard), compiler internals (Lexer, Thunk, Sigil, Monad), git plumbing (Bisect, Cherry, Stash), and ML vocabulary (Tensor, Gradient, Epoch). Dropped a few candidates for bad connotations before checking: Nonce (a real cryptographic term, but also offensive British slang — not worth the risk), and Deadlock / Segfault / Panic (technically on-brand for a coding tool, but all read as "something broke," the opposite of "create anything").

.com was a clean sweep of "taken" again — for real reasons: a follow-up B2B SaaS collision check (see Collision Check) found that 24 of these 26 names are already used as a brand by a real, currently-operating tech/SaaS company — only Bisect and Zombie came back clean. This is a strong pattern: meaningful, memorable CS jargon is exactly what other technical founders reach for too. Seven names — Sigil, Monad, Quorum, Raft, Checkpoint, Vertex, Stash — are also taken on every single TLD checked, including .build and .codes.

Collision check & fallback domains

Every name in Categories 5 and 6 (76 total: 26 + 50) was searched individually for an existing, currently-operating B2B SaaS/cybersecurity/fintech/AI company already using it as a brand — not just documentation mentioning the term generically. This check was NOT run on Categories 1–4 (the plain-English "create anything" words, dev-term words, coined OpenClaw/T3-style names, or suffix remixes) — those are either too generic to attribute to one company (Forge, Craft) or invented compounds unlikely to collide (Q3Code, ForgeClaw), but they haven't been formally verified the same way.

Result: 61 of 76 advanced/obscure terms (80%) collide with a real company — overwhelmingly cybersecurity, fintech, and AI/B2B-SaaS firms, exactly the pattern you flagged. For every collision, 4 prefixes (Get-/Try-/Use-/Run-) and 4 suffixes (-ly/-us/-ify/-io) were checked on .com to find a clean alternative. 17 names had no clean fallback among those 8 options — for those, either accept the collision risk, try a different modifier, or drop the name.
